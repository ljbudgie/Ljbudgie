## The problem — and my answer

Institutions hide behind automation. *"The computer says no"* — without a single human ever looking at the facts.

I'm a disabled builder who got tired of that answer. So I built the **Burgess Principle**: one operational test — **was a human member of the team able to personally review the specific facts of my specific situation?** — and the open-source ecosystem to enforce it. Outcomes resolve to **SOVEREIGN**, **NULL**, or **AMBIGUOUS** when an institution hides behind process language instead of answering directly.

## Three intersecting layers

**1. Clinical & Data Layer — Living Hearing Profile**  
The audiogram is no longer locked in clinic software. OpenHear v1.5.0 moves the profile onto the wearer's device: dynamic, versioned, and context-layered. Every revision from the 2021 baseline recomputes to matching hashes on a cryptographically audited Git ledger, backed by a 1,611-test green CI suite.

**2. Hardware & Haptic Layer — Software-defined environmental awareness**  
Critical sounds (knock, alarm, vehicle) are classified locally and translated into calibrated vibration patterns on a wristband, shaped by the Living Hearing Profile. Lightweight haptic triggers replace raw audio streaming, fitting low-latency protocols such as Auracast / Bluetooth LE Audio.

**3. Regulatory & Legal Layer — Named human review**  
The Burgess Principle (UK Certification Mark UK00004343685) operationalises the “meaningful human involvement” requirement in the EU AI Act, UK GDPR Articles 22A–22D, and the Equality Act 2010. Iris routes escalations to named officers. The framework has been field-tested against energy suppliers, platforms, and regulators.
